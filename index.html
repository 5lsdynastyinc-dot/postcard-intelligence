England changed the QTS route for Nigeria- and Ghana-trained teachers

The direct recognition service changed on 9 September 2026. That is not the same as a UK-wide teaching ban.

Confirmed 24 September 2026 5 min read Live deployment test
What changed England changed one direct QTS recognition route. It did not create a UK-wide teaching ban.
Briefings

Three things to know

01

Direct route changed

From 9 September 2026, teachers trained in Nigeria, Ghana or India can no longer use England's Apply for QTS direct-recognition service.

02

Not a UK-wide ban

The verified evidence does not support saying those teachers have been banned from teaching across the United Kingdom or that existing QTS has been cancelled.

03

Context decides the next route

Training, experience, location, employment and right-to-work status matter before an individual pathway can be assessed.

Full intelligence

What changed

Teachers who trained in Nigeria, Ghana or India are no longer eligible to use England's Apply for QTS direct-recognition service from 9 September 2026.

The confirmed change is about eligibility for that service in England. It should not be compressed into a claim that Nigerian or Ghanaian teachers have been banned from teaching across the UK.

What did not happen

The change does not itself cancel existing QTS, does not make QTS a visa, and does not establish that every alternative professional route is unavailable.

Employment, professional recognition and immigration permission are separate questions and should be checked separately.

Why the route changed

The Department for Education says countries using the service must support reliable checks of professional standing and work-history references. It reports that more than 70% of applications from Ghana and Nigeria used public email addresses for work-history references rather than official school domains.

How large was the route?

For the 2024–25 academic year, DfE transparency data records 1,076 applications from Nigeria, with 364 resulting in QTS and 599 declined. Ghana recorded 1,067 applications, with 196 resulting in QTS and 749 declined.

Those totals do not necessarily equal awards plus declines because some applications may have been under review or withdrawn.

What an affected teacher should check next

Before spending money or changing plans, establish the facts that determine the person's route: training country, current location, qualification, teaching experience, subject or age phase, employment position, and immigration or right-to-work status.
